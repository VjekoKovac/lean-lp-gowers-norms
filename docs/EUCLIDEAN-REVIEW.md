## Proof and external inputs

The scalar cube calculation pairs its vertices and completes the square. Tonelli on the product of the coordinate measures gives the exact multidimensional Gaussian moments. The finite positive critical norm of the test function allows cancellation when deriving the necessary lower bound on the constant. A Gram decomposition and the determinant change of variables extend the equality to every positive-definite Gaussian. Null changes preserve the cube correlation for each fixed tuple of increments. These results hold unconditionally.

The sharp upper bound is unconditional. Yi Han’s formal proof of the geometric Brascamp–Lieb inequality gives constant one for a finite coisometric frame on Hilbert spaces. At Young output exponent $s>1$, put $a^2=1/(s+1)$ and $b^2=s/(s+1)$. The maps $(w,z)\mapsto -aw+bz$, $(w,z)\mapsto aw+bz$, and $(w,z)\mapsto w$ have weights $(s+1)/(2s)$, $(s+1)/(2s)$, and $(s-1)/s$. Their coisometry and weighted resolution identities hold in every finite Euclidean dimension. Rescaling the third test function by $2a$ and applying the two Euclidean Jacobians gives the one-dimensional factor $2s^{s/2}/(s+1)^{(s+1)/2}$ at the raw $s$th-moment level. These are the project proofs in `YoungFrame`, `YoungChangeVariables`, and `GeometricYoung`. The dual test is the autocorrelation to the power $s-1$, with zero and infinite moment cases handled separately. At $s=1$ the identity follows from translation invariance and Tonelli. [Yi Han, geometric Brascamp–Lieb, pinned source](https://github.com/hanyi162013-Yihan/geometric-brascamp-lieb/tree/9155169a5ba8f7db77c01c446430e50c0daceb7d)

Each Young step acts on a lower-dimensional cube product, raising the source exponent from $p$ to $p+1$ with an inner power $2p/(p+1)$. Multiplication of the constants gives the sharp upper bound in the table. Together with the Gaussian lower test and affine invariance, `euclidean_sharp_estimate` and `ae_gaussian_equality` establish the inequality, optimality and attainment by every Gaussian. A separate consequence of Theorem 1.2, `euclidean_strict_bound`, gives a uniform improvement over one for all positive Euclidean dimensions, even for interior exponents below one.

The full equality classification is unconditional. Section 4 descends through the Young steps to an ordinary Young extremizer. The last exponent is $s=p+d-1>1$, including when $p=1$. For the repeated input $U=f^t$, with $t=2^{d-1}p/s$, the Young exponent satisfies $t\,2s/(s+1)=q$. Raising a positive Gaussian to the reciprocal power and restoring its amplitude recover the Gaussian representative of $f$.

The equality case for ordinary Young is proved in this repository. The dual test from the sharp estimate produces an extremal triple for the geometric Brascamp–Lieb inequality. To analyze it, multiply each row input by a translated heat kernel. The geometric frame identity makes the product of the row kernels the Euclidean heat kernel in the source. The resulting pointwise inequality compares the heat convolution of the product with the product of the heat convolutions. For extremal data the two sides have the same integral. Continuity yields pointwise equality at every positive time, including for unbounded-support inputs. Differentiating this identity and using the heat equation gives zero geometric defect. Its sum-of-squares expansion forces the two copies of the smoothed logarithmic gradient to have translation-invariant first differences. Consequently this gradient is affine, and integration on lines gives a quadratic logarithm.

An approximate-identity argument gives a sequence of positive heat times along which the smoothed densities converge almost everywhere to the original density. The logarithms are quadratic polynomials. Their restrictions to a positive-measure set determine their coefficients, because the zero set of a nonzero polynomial has zero Lebesgue measure. Finite-dimensional closure gives an exponential quadratic as the a.e. limit. A finite positive moment forces positive definiteness of its negative quadratic term. Completing the square produces exactly the centered, positive-amplitude Gaussian in the Challenge. The modules `YoungExtremalLift`, `YoungHeatEquality`, `YoungFrameRigidity`, `HeatApproximation`, `QuadraticPrimitive`, `GaussianLogClosure`, `GaussianRigidity` and `SharpYoungEquality` implement these steps. The declaration `euclideanPartA` proves the bound, the optimal constant and the two-way characterization together.

## Uniform Gaussian stability

Section 4 of the source paper applies Christ’s Euclidean near-extremizer theorem after its last Young step. Only a nonnegative diagonal input and an output exponent $s=p+d-1\ge2$ occur in that step. The Lean proof supplies this case by the following argument. [Christ, 2019](https://doi.org/10.4171/rmi/1055)

Let $r=2s/(s+1)$, $s'=s/(s-1)$, $r'=2s/(s-1)$ and let $K$ denote the sharp **$s$-th moment** for $\|F\|_r=1$. Put $R(t)=\int F(y)F(y+t)\,dy$. The normalized Euler map on nonnegative inputs is
$$
T(F)(x)=\left(K^{-1}\int R(t)^{s-1}F(x+t)\,dt\right)^{r'-1}.
$$
Testing the dual score against $F$ gives $K^{-1}\int R^s$. Sharp trilinear Young bounds that score in $L^{r'}$ by one. Quantitative near equality in Hölder therefore gives $\|T(F_j)-F_j\|_r\to0$ for every normalized extremizing sequence. The two powers in $T$ extend to signed powers on real Lebesgue spaces. Their orders are $s-1\ge1$ and $r'-1>1$, including the identity map for the first power at $s=2$. The signed correlation maps have the same integral formula almost everywhere and are bounded bilinear maps between the required spaces.

At a fixed centered Gaussian the signed Euler map has a compact strict Fréchet derivative. Differentiation leaves two outer correlation terms. Multiplication by the Gaussian derivative weight makes both operators compact, by approximation of their kernels in the appropriate mixed norms by finite sums of separated kernels. Let $L$ be this derivative. In a sufficiently small ball, $T-L$ has Lipschitz constant at most one half. For approximate fixed points $u_j$ in that ball, take a subsequence along which $Lu_j$ converges. Then $y_j=(u_j-Tu_j)+Lu_j$ also converges, and the Lipschitz estimate gives $\|u_j-u_k\|_r\le 2\|y_j-y_k\|_r$. Completeness supplies a strong limit.

For a general nonnegative normalized near-extremizer, lift to three nonnegative unit-mass rows of the geometric Young frame and evolve the rows by the heat flow. The near-maximal lower bound survives at every time, and the critical root of the first row gives a continuous path of normalized Young near-extremizers. Its initial point recovers the input in $L^r$. At large heat times its distance to the orbit of the normalized Gaussian tends to zero. If uniform stability failed, one could choose extremizing inputs staying a fixed positive distance from that orbit. Each path would cross a smaller common level of the distance. By affine invariance the crossing profiles can be put in the local compactness ball without changing their distance or objective. A convergent subsequence has unit norm and attains equality, by continuity of the objective. The equality classification puts the limit in the Gaussian orbit, contradicting the positive distance of the sequence. Christ gives this heat-flow crossing argument for nonnegative triples in §3 of his later work. His perturbative estimate uses a Hermite expansion. Here the compact derivative suffices for the qualitative conclusion. [Christ, *Young’s inequality sharpened*, §3, p. 266, Lemma 6 and the following argument](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-72058-2_7)

Finally the source's power transfer and amplitude normalization construct a Gaussian $g$ on the original coordinates satisfying $\|g\|_q=\|f\|_q$ and $\|f-g\|_q\le\eta\|f\|_q$ for the requested uniform deficit. The source uses the stronger literature theorem for unrestricted pairs of functions. The route above formalizes the smaller assertion needed here. The declaration `diagonal_young_stability_above_two` proves the resulting diagonal Young theorem, including $s=2$. The closed declaration `euclideanTheorem` applies it at the last exponent, and `Solution.lean` exposes the result to Comparator as `LpGowers.theorem_1_3`. [Kovač and Rogers, arXiv:2609.08916v2, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08916v2)

The statement preserves the unrestricted measurable nonnegative bound, the minimum over all admissible constants, and the two-way classification for finite-norm inputs nonzero modulo null sets. Direct expansion gives $q=2^d p/(d+p)$ and $K=(2^{2d}p^p/(d+p)^{d+p})^{n/(2^{d+1}p)}$. Testing the endpoint $p=1$ in the formulas leaves the final Young exponent at $s=d>1$ for every allowed $d$. The Gaussian witness includes a positive amplitude, arbitrary center, symmetry and strict positivity of the quadratic form in every nonzero direction. The hypothesis in (b) measures the deficit relative to $K$ and the full input norm. The order of quantifiers gives a single threshold for all inputs after fixing $d,p,n$ and the error tolerance. The norm of the witness must equal the norm of the input exactly.

For part (a), the proof iterates the ordinary sharp Young estimate, evaluates the Gaussian test, and transports it under affine changes. For the equality converse, the normalized last Young step attains its own sharp bound. The geometric Brascamp–Lieb implementation regularizes the density by heat flow and proves the logarithm is quadratic. Finite-dimensional closure recovers the original density almost everywhere, and its integrability forces a positive-definite Gaussian. Taking a positive power and restoring the input amplitude gives the witness for the original function. This provides a Lean proof of the Gaussian rigidity step that the paper obtains from previously established equality theory.
