## Checks of the supporting arguments

The reviewer inspected the proof strategies for the subgroup indicator, the autocorrelation marginal, scalar Gaussian evaluation, finite-exponent interpolation, and finite energy comparison. The subgroup formula follows by testing the empty vertex and the singleton vertices, then using closure under addition. The marginal integrates a translated copy of one lower-dimensional cube integrand, so Tonelli and translation invariance give the square of its integral. Completing the square in all scalar cube vertices leaves the shift $\tfrac12\sum_i h_i$ and the term $\tfrac14\sum_i h_i^2$. Finite-exponent interpolation uses Hölder with weights $p\theta/a$ and $p(1-\theta)/b$, which sum to one. For the energy comparison, at fixed increments the number of valid base points is at most $|A|$, giving $E_{d,p}(A)\le |A|^{p-1}E_{d,1}(A)$ term by term.

The Euclidean proof factors the standard Gaussian over the $n$ coordinates and the $d$ increments. The formula for its critical norm is positive and finite, so dividing the estimate by that value proves that every admissible constant is at least the displayed $K$. The affine extension represents a real symmetric positive-definite quadratic form as a Gram form, changes variables by an invertible real linear map, and accounts for translation, amplitude, and the determinant of the change of variables. These computations yield the equality for all a.e. Gaussians in the source class. The Gaussian computation supplies the minimality part of the subsequent universal upper bound. The converse equality classification follows from the heat-flow argument described below.

Independent arithmetic spot checks used Python integers and `fractions.Fraction`. The square-completion identity was tested by direct enumeration of the vertices for $d=0,\ldots,5$, $h_i=(-1)^i(i+1)/3$ with $i$ counted from zero, and $x\in\{-3/5,1/11\}$. The ten relative exponent identities $1=\theta/(d/(2d-1))+(1-\theta)/2$ for $d=2,\ldots,11$ were also checked exactly. These finite tests provide independent checks of notation and normalization. ## Proof obligations at the November 24 baseline

Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, and 1.5 and the full part (a) of 1.3 have unconditional Lean proofs. At that stage, the missing mathematical step for 1.3(b) was uniform control of near-extremizers of ordinary diagonal Euclidean Young modulo affine symmetries. In particular, equality classification applies after obtaining a strongly convergent normalized sequence. The positive-time heat-flow defect alone permits extremizers escaping by translation, dilation or changes of eccentricity.

`YoungIntervalCompact.lean` proves one local compactness step from Christ’s argument. Indicators of intervals with both endpoints in a fixed bounded window depend continuously on their endpoints in strong $L^p(\mathbb R)$ for $1\le p<\infty$. The closed convex hull of this compact family is compact, so it contains all probability averages of these interval indicators. If each original level set differs from its interval by measure at most $\delta$, averaging the indicators has strong $L^p$ error at most $\delta^{1/p}$. If profiles admit arbitrarily accurate strong approximations by scaled interval averages, with a fixed window and scale for each tolerance, they have a strongly convergent subsequence. The reviewer wrote this auxiliary module. An argument following this interval route would also have to derive such approximations for the original near-extremizers and control affine normalization in all dimensions.
